00Auckland at a Glance
Auckland, New Zealand's largest and most dynamic city, stands as a beacon of opportunity for investors seeking stable and long-term growth in the residential property sector. With a population exceeding 1.7 million and projections indicating a rise to over 2 million by the early 2030s, Auckland accounts for approximately 34% of the nation's population. This demographic surge underscores the city's pivotal role in New Zealand's economic and urban development.
Auckland's economy is a powerhouse, contributing significantly to the national GDP. In 2022, the property industry alone had a direct impact of $13.0 billion on the regional economy, with the wider economic impact reaching $36.7 billion. The city's economic landscape is marked by a diverse mix of industries, including technology, finance, and manufacturing, providing a robust foundation for sustained growth.
Auckland's combination of economic vitality, strategic infrastructure investments, and demographic trends creates a compelling case for residential property investment. The city's commitment to sustainable urban development and its proactive approach to accommodating population growth ensure a resilient and attractive environment for investors seeking long-term returns in the residential sector.

01A Global City
Auckland — New Zealand’s Only True Global City
As the economic and cultural capital of New Zealand, Auckland is home to over 1.7 million residents, accounting for over one-third of the country’s total population. Unlike comparable countries such as Australia — where growth is distributed across Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane — Auckland is New Zealand’s sole megacity, making it the primary destination for migration, investment, and employment.
Source: Infometrics
Unmatched Population Growth & Urban Demand
Auckland’s population is forecast to surpass 2 million by 2035, driven by both strong natural growth and consistent migration inflows. In 2022–23 alone, New Zealand welcomed over 110,000 new migrants, with the majority settling in Auckland — further intensifying the demand for high-quality rental housing in key growth precincts such as Albany.
Source: OECD, International Migration Database and Statistics New Zealand
A Globally Competitive Rental Market
Auckland is now firmly positioned among the world’s least affordable housing markets. According to the 2021 Demographia International Housing Affordability survey, it ranked as the 4th most unaffordable city globally out of 92 major urban centres, with house prices now 10 times the median household income—up from 5.4 in 2004. As the barrier to homeownership continues to rise, the demand for long-term, high-quality rental accommodation has become not only consistent but structural. In this context, Build to Rent (BTR) is emerging as a necessary response to the market.
Source: Westpac NZ Home Ownership Report
02Migration & Investment
New Zealand — A Magnet for Migration & Investment
New Zealand consistently ranks as one of the world’s most desirable immigration destinations, valued for its safety, education, healthcare and natural beauty. Auckland remains the first choice for new arrivals, with its diversity, employment opportunities, and connectivity. With no comparable alternative urban centre in the country, Auckland will continue to concentrate the lion’s share of New Zealand’s population and housing demand.
03Infrastructure & Environment
Strategic Infrastructure and Urban Development
Auckland is undergoing transformative infrastructure developments aimed at enhancing connectivity and urban livability. The City Rail Link (CRL), a 3.5 km double-track rail tunnel, is set to revolutionize the city's public transport network upon its completion in 2026. Additionally, the Auckland International Airport is investing NZ$1.4 billion in capital programs, including the construction of a new domestic terminal, further cementing the city's status as a key regional hub.
Source: Auckland Airport · industry.aucklandnz.com/invest
A Globally Competitive Investment and Tax Environment
New Zealand ranks first in the world for ease of doing business, according to the World Bank. There are no restrictions, for example, on the transfer of capital, profits, interest, dividends or royalties into or out of the country. New Zealand also offers an investor-friendly tax regime: there is no capital gains tax, no stamp duty, and no payroll tax. With a flat 28% corporate income tax, the country remains a globally competitive destination for institutional investors seeking stable, long-term returns. The Overseas Investment (Build-to-Rent and Similar Rental Developments) Amendment Act 2025 specifically removes barriers for foreign investment into BTR.
Building Industry: A Leading Force in Auckland’s Fastest-Growing Sector
Auckland’s economy is thriving across multiple high-value sectors, offering investors exposure to long-term, sustainable growth. Construction and Engineering is Auckland’s most dynamic and expansive industry—growing by over 72% in the past decade, with sustained momentum across public infrastructure and private development. Build to Rent (BTR) sits squarely within this high-growth sector, offering institutional investors access to one of New Zealand’s most resilient, scalable, and future-proofed asset classes.
Source: Infometrics · industry.aucklandnz.com (Statistics New Zealand)
